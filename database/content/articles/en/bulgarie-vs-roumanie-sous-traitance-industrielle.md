## Two complementary destinations

Bulgaria and Romania are regularly mentioned side by side as preferred destinations for industrial subcontracting in Europe. Both EU members, they share common strengths – competitive costs, skilled labour, European standards – but have distinct profiles depending on sectors and needs.

## Production costs

Bulgaria has the lowest labour costs in the EU: €10.9 per hour in industry in 2025, versus €12.6 in Romania (Insee / Eurostat). Its minimum wage is also lower than Romania's, which means slightly more competitive cost prices for labour-intensive production. Romania, whose economy has grown faster, is seeing its costs gradually move closer to the European average, while remaining far below France or Germany.

## Industrial base and skills

Romania has a denser industrial base, inherited from its large former manufacturing sector. It is particularly recognised for automotive, heavy engineering and industrial equipment. Bulgaria, for its part, excels in fine metalwork, boilermaking, steel structures and high-value finished products.

## Logistics and accessibility

Both capitals are well connected to European hubs. Romania has a Black Sea coastline with the port of Constanța, useful for some flows. Bulgaria also offers sea access via the Black Sea ports of Varna and Burgas, connected to the Mediterranean through the Bosphorus.
